Spoilt for Choice

Selected factors influencing the poll numbers of German parties

Bastien Chevallier & Johannes Frohnmeyer

This presentation was created during a 1.5-day hackathon at KIT. It looks at political polling data, searches for biases by polling institute and for the influence of economic indicators (or so we intended).
“If the federal election were held next Sunday, which of the following parties would you vote for?”
(“Sonntagsfrage”)
The classic question most pollsters ask, called the Sonntagsfrage (“Sunday question”).

Dataset 2

2002 – Jan 2026
Number of polls: 5,079 (Bundestag)
Source: wahlrecht.de
Our main dataset: it covers a long period, many polling institutes, and only national polls.
Pretty animation :)
Just a reminder of how much the window size matters when using rolling averages. The window grows from 5 days to almost 14 years. I can’t think of a situation where a rolling average is adequate preprocessing.

Do institutes differ?

Deviation of each institute’s polls from the 30-day average of all polls.
A mean away from zero hints at a “house effect”.

This method kind of sucks, since a bias shared by all institutes won’t show up. On top of that, institutes that poll a lot (Forsa, Verian, INSA) shift the baseline for everyone, and each institute is compared with an average that includes its own polls. Still interesting though.

Both series trend over time, so this is not evidence of causation.

GDP has only 23 data points, and the other indicators have a lot of points that basically only rise. We could have taken any other data source with a fairly constant upward trend and shown a big correlation with the AfD (positive) or the CDU/CSU and SPD (negative). Of course we didn’t know that when we started the analysis. You’re always smarter afterwards.
